9 Magnetism

9.1 Magnetic fields

Key words: compass · like poles · magnet · magnetic · magnetic field · magnetic field lines

The magnetic field

A magnet is something that will attract magnetic materials. Magnetic materials include the metals iron, steel, nickel and cobalt.

You have probably used a magnet to pick up paperclips.

Magnets have two poles, north and south. On diagrams they are shown with the letters N and S.

When a paperclip is close to one of the poles of a magnet, the paperclip is attracted to the magnet. As you move the paperclip further away, it stops being attracted.

The paperclip is attracted to a magnet only when it is inside the magnetic field of that magnet.

Remember: a magnetic field is the area around a magnet where the effects of the magnet can be detected.

A magnetic field surrounds every magnet. The magnetic field of a magnet is strongest at the poles.

Detecting a magnetic field

You can detect a magnetic field in two ways.

  • See whether a magnetic object moves because it is attracted.
  • Use a compass. A compass contains a magnetised needle that is free to turn. The needle turns and points along the magnetic field.

Some mobile devices, such as phones, have compass apps.

Magnetic field lines

You can draw magnetic field lines around a magnet to represent the magnetic field. The rules are:

  • magnetic field lines join opposite poles
  • the field lines have arrows that point from N to S
  • field lines must not touch each other
  • field lines must not cross each other.

Field lines also tell you how strong the field is. The magnetic field is strongest where the lines are closest together.

If one magnet is stronger than another, the field of the stronger magnet is different in two ways:

  • all the field lines are closer together
  • the field lines extend further away from the magnet.

A compass placed in a magnetic field points in the direction of the field lines.

Magnetic fields interacting

You already know about the forces between two magnets.

Poles brought togetherForce
Two north polesrepel
Two south polesrepel
A north pole and a south poleattract

This force is caused by the magnetic fields of the two magnets.

The field lines between these two magnets all point in the same direction, so there is a force of attraction between the magnets.

The field lines in the space directly between these two magnets point in opposite directions, so the magnets repel and try to move away from each other.

Measuring the strength of a magnetic field

One way to compare magnets is to find how far away a magnet can still attract a paperclip. The further away the paperclip is picked up, the stronger the magnetic field.

Sprinkling iron filings on a sheet of paper over a magnet shows the field pattern. The filings crowd together at the poles, where the field is strongest.

9.2 The Earth as a giant magnet

Key words: geographic north pole · magnetic north · naturally occurring · navigate

Lodestone and the first compasses

Around 4000 years ago a Greek shepherd called Magnes noticed that the iron nails in his shoes stuck to one particular type of rock. The rock was called lodestone. It contains a substance later named magnetite, which is a naturally occurring magnet — one that is not made by people.

The Chinese discovered that a small needle of lodestone could be floated on water. When it was free to float, the needle always turned to point the same way: one end towards the north, the other towards the south. This was the invention of the magnetic compass.

The compass needle points to a place close to the Earth's north pole, but not exactly to it. That place is called magnetic north. Magnetic north moves very slowly and is currently in the Arctic Ocean, north of Alaska.

The compass was important because it let people navigate across oceans and deserts with less chance of getting lost. Even today, with satellite navigation (satnav), ships and aeroplanes still carry magnetic compasses. Satnav systems do not use the Earth's magnetic field.

Some animals use the Earth's magnetic field to navigate over long distances.

The Earth's magnetic field

Notice that the field lines around the Earth point towards the Earth's north pole. Field lines always point from north to south, so the north pole of the Earth is actually a magnetic south pole.

Remember: magnetic north means the magnetic pole close to the geographic north pole; magnetic south means the magnetic pole close to the geographic south pole.

The geographic north and south poles are the parts of the Earth through which the spin axis passes.

The Earth's magnetic field also causes natural lights in the night sky close to the north and south poles. They appear when particles arriving from the Sun reach the stronger parts of the Earth's magnetic field.

Origin of the Earth's magnetic field

People once thought the Earth was made almost entirely from magnetic rocks. It is now known that the high temperatures deep inside the Earth would make rocks lose any magnetism they had.

Scientists also know that the Earth's magnetic field has reversed in the past. The last change was around 500 000 years ago, when south really was north.

The origin of the field is the Earth's core, although scientists have still to discover the exact reason.

  • The core is mostly made from iron, and the heat generated there keeps creating a magnetic field.
  • The core also contains some nickel, which is another magnetic metal.
  • Movement in the liquid outer core explains why the magnetic poles move slowly and have occasionally reversed.

Magnetic north moves at a speed of about 60 km per year.

Runway numbers sometimes have to be repainted because magnetic north has moved.

9.3 Electromagnets

Key words: coil · core · electromagnet · magnetic · magnetised · permanent magnets

Properties of magnetic materials

A material is described as magnetic if it is attracted to a magnet. Magnetic materials include iron, nickel and cobalt. Steel is another common magnetic metal; steel is a mixture that contains a large proportion of iron.

Magnetic materials can be magnetised, which means turned into a magnet. Every magnet you have used was made by magnetising a magnetic material.

Those magnets are called permanent magnets because their magnetic field is always there. You cannot switch it off and on again.

One way to magnetise a magnetic material is by using electricity. A magnet made this way is called an electromagnet.

Making an electromagnet

An electromagnet is made by wrapping a wire around a magnetic material such as iron.

  • The wire that is wrapped around is called the coil.
  • The material in the middle of the coil is called the core.

When current passes through the coil, the magnetic material becomes magnetised. When the current is switched off, the magnetic material loses most of its magnetism.

You can build the same electromagnet from a large iron nail, a coil of insulated wire, a cell and a switch.

Only the plastic coating of the wire should touch the nail, and the electromagnet should be switched on for the shortest possible time so the cell lasts.

Poles of an electromagnet

An electromagnet has two poles, just like a bar magnet. There are two simple ways to find out which pole is which.

  • Use a magnetic compass. A compass points along magnetic field lines, so it points towards the south pole.
  • Use a bar magnet with known poles. Opposite poles attract and like poles repel, so bringing the bar magnet close shows which pole is which.

You can reverse the poles of an electromagnet in one of two ways:

  • wrap the coil around in the opposite direction
  • reverse the connections on the cell or power supply.

Applications of electromagnets

Electromagnets are used wherever a permanent magnet would not be useful, because an electromagnet can be switched off.

A fire door is held open by an electromagnet connected to the fire alarm. When the alarm goes off the magnet is switched off and the door closes.

An MRI scanner produces images from inside the human body. MRI stands for magnetic resonance imaging.

An electromagnet sorting scrap attracts iron and steel and leaves the non-magnetic metals behind. Common non-magnetic metals include copper, aluminium and zinc. Once the magnetic metals have been lifted and moved, switching off the magnet drops them.

In a toaster, pushing the handle down makes an electromagnet hold a metal basket down. A timer turns the electromagnet off and the basket pops up with the toast.

An electric bell uses an electromagnet to move the hammer. When the electromagnet is on, the hammer is pulled onto the bell. That movement breaks the circuit, so the hammer moves away, the circuit becomes complete again and the hammer is pulled back. This repeats until the power supply is turned off. Electric bells are used in schools, as fire alarms and as door bells.

Electric motors use electromagnets to change electrical energy into kinetic energy.

9.4 Investigating electromagnets

Key words: factors · soft iron

Strength of electromagnets

Topic 9.3 listed the three things needed to make an electromagnet:

  • a coil of wire
  • a magnetic core inside the coil
  • an electric current flowing in the coil.

These three things are also the factors that affect how strong the electromagnet is. A factor is another word for a variable that will affect something.

FactorEffect on strength
Number of turns in the coilThe more turns, the stronger the electromagnet
Material of the coreIron and some types of steel make the strongest electromagnets
Current in the coilThe greater the current, the stronger the electromagnet

Three ways to make an electromagnet stronger

Increase the number of turns on the coil. Keep the current and the core the same.

Use more cells to increase the current. Keep the number of turns and the core the same.

Use a soft iron core in place of a steel core. Keep the number of turns and the current the same.

Remember: each of these is a fair test — only one factor is changed and the other two are kept the same.

Soft iron

Soft iron is not soft in the way modelling clay is soft. Soft iron is the term for iron that is easily magnetised and also easily demagnetised. Demagnetised means it has lost its magnetism.

In 2019, scientists broke the world record for the strongest electromagnet. They made one 4500 times stronger than a school bar magnet. It uses more electricity than three million electric lamps.

Comparing electromagnets

The strength of an electromagnet can be measured by the force it exerts on a magnetic material. The easiest way to do this is to count how many magnetic objects the electromagnet can lift and hold.
